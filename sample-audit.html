The story

Uncle Ott’s is two siblings, their grandfather’s recipe, and eight years of grating fresh horseradish that makes grown chefs sit down. It started at a farmers-market card table. It is now 41 grocery doors built store by store, one regional distributor, a webshop shipping 3-packs nationwide, and the Root Club — 214 members who get a quarterly box and whatever the siblings are testing that season.

The product wins awards. The office is a laptop next to the grating room, an inbox with 4,000 unread emails, and a binder labeled DISTRIBUTOR that lives in the delivery van. It is the only office where crying at your email might just be the horseradish.

The recipe has never missed. The follow-through misses plenty.

FINDING 01Wholesale · quiet account

The grocer reordered every three weeks for two years. Then it didn’t.

9 WEEKSof silence from a clockwork account
What the records show

The account placed 34 consecutive orders, normally 19 to 24 days apart. Nine weeks have passed without another order, and no follow-up is recorded. The account still says active.

What the records do not show

The order history does not explain why the account stopped. A buyer change is possible, but it is not proved by these records.

Source trail

Wholesale order history · prior 24 months
Account record and contact log · through the Audit cutoff date

Why it needs attention

A reliable account crossed its normal reorder window and nobody followed up. That is the miss—without calling the account lost or inventing revenue at risk.

What VNMSFX would build

A system that flags any account past its own ordering rhythm and shows the last order, the history, the owner and a drafted check-in — a weekly list of warm accounts, before they cool all the way.

FINDING 02Ads · direct to consumer

The ads kept running. The flavor most first-time customers chose was gone.

6 DAYSads kept running while the most common first-order flavor was unavailable
What the records show

In the prior 90 days, Classic appeared in 68 of 74 first orders. Classic was unavailable for six days while the same ads kept sending people to the product page. First orders were lower during those six days.

What the records do not show

The records do not prove that every missing first order was caused by the stockout. They prove that paid traffic kept arriving while the most common first-order flavor was unavailable.

Source trail

Shopify first-order export · 90-day window
Product availability history · six affected days
Meta active-campaign list · same six days

Why it needs attention

Paid traffic was still sending new shoppers to a page missing the flavor most first-time customers had chosen. The owner needs to decide whether to pause, redirect or restock before the same mismatch continues.

What VNMSFX would build

A system that checks active campaigns against the agreed product and inventory records and flags the mismatch while the ads are still spending.

FINDING 03Social · what to post

The posts that lined up with more site visits and first orders are the posts they stopped making.

3 VS 40recipe reels posted last quarter, versus label glamour shots
What the records show

Recipe reels appeared during periods with higher site visits and first orders. The label photographs did not show the same pattern.

What the records do not show

That pattern does not prove the recipe reels caused the orders. The next step is a tracked test that compares the formats under the same measurement rules.

Source trail

Social post log · prior quarter
Site visits and first-order report · same agreed comparison windows
Link/tag coverage check · attribution limits shown

Why it needs attention

The team is producing the format that looks good in the feed without testing the format that lines up with more visits and first orders.

What VNMSFX would build

A system that classifies every post by format, lines each format up against an agreed traffic-and-order window, separates real attribution from coincidence, and tells the calendar which formats deserve another test.

FINDING 04Wholesale · deductions

The distributor deducted for short cases. The proof was in three places.

9 DAYSuntil the first dispute deadline closes
What the records show

The distributor claimed 17 short cases across four shipments. Signed delivery receipts show full counts on three of those shipments. The earliest dispute deadline closes in nine days.

What the records do not show

The receipts make those three deductions disputable. They do not prove the distributor will reverse them, and they are not recovered money.

Source trail

Distributor deduction statements · four purchase orders
3PL pick records · four shipments
Signed delivery receipts · four shipments
Distributor dispute terms · 90-day window

Why it needs attention

The evidence exists now. If nobody assembles it before the deadline, the chance to challenge the deduction expires.

What VNMSFX would build

A system that reads each deduction, checks it against the dispute deadline, gathers the matching purchase order, pick record and delivery receipt, and prepares one evidence package for the siblings to review.

FINDING 05The Root Club · win-backs

Every lapsed member gets the same 20% off — whatever the reason they left.

20%the one offer sent to every canceled or paused member
What the records show

Nine members lapsed after failed card payments. Other members named shipping cost or intentionally canceled. Every group currently receives the same 20% offer.

What the records do not show

The records do not prove which message or offer will bring anyone back. A card-update reminder and a shipping offer are tests—not guaranteed recoveries.

Source trail

Subscription status and cancellation-reason export
Failed-payment log
Win-back campaign send and offer rules

Why it needs attention

Different problems are receiving the same expensive answer. The next step is to test smaller, reason-specific responses before assuming everybody needs 20% off.

What VNMSFX would build

A system that separates lapsed members by the recorded reason, prepares the approved response for each test group and compares each approach against actual reactivation. It does not assume which offer will work.
