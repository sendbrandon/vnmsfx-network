Confirmed loss

$13,000 / YEAR

Money that already left, at order level, and leaves again every year until something changes. Computed from your own order and cost records — not modelled.

−$5,600/YEAR

The fall promo code never stopped running.

FALL24 · campaign ended 12 months ago · 412 orders since · avg discount $13.60 (20% of $68 AOV) · 412 × $13.60 = $5,603

ONE CLICK
WORKING PAPER
Category
Confirmed contribution loss. A discount comes off contribution one-for-one.
Source
Shopify → Discounts report, code FALL24; order-level export cross-checked against the campaign brief that set the end date.
Period
12 months, from the campaign's stated end date to today.
Formula
412 orders × $13.60 avg discount = $5,603
rounded down to $5,600
Assumption
The soft spot in this finding: it treats every discounted order as one that would have converted at full price. 71% of these orders arrived from coupon-aggregator sites — people who had already decided to buy, then went looking for a code. Those are the recoverable ones. The other 29% are less certain.
Confidence
High on the amount given away ($5,603 is billed fact). Medium on how much returns to you after the code is switched off.
Dedup
Excludes all WELCOME15 orders — those are counted once, in the stacking finding below. No order appears in two findings.
Action
Deactivate the code. One switch, no trade-off, nothing downstream depends on it.

−$7,400/YEAR

Your best seller loses $1.90 every time the welcome code stacks with free shipping.

Full price: +$6.10 contribution/order · stacked: −$1.90 · 3,900 stacked orders in 12 mo · 3,900 × $1.90 = $7,410

YOUR CALL
WORKING PAPER
Category
Confirmed contribution loss, at order level.
Source
Shopify order export (line-item discounts + shipping) joined to unit COGS from QuickBooks; 3PL pick-and-pack rate from the fulfilment invoice.
Period
12 months.
Margin
Contribution = unit price − COGS − pick/pack − outbound shipping − payment fees. It does not carry overhead, salaries or software.
Formula
full price: $6.10 contribution
+ WELCOME15 (−$5.10) + free ship (−$2.90)
= −$1.90 per stacked order
3,900 × $1.90 = $7,410 → $7,400
Confidence
High. Every one of the 3,900 orders is individually identified — this is counted, not estimated.
Dedup
Excludes the 412 FALL24 orders above. The two codes cannot combine, so the sets do not overlap.
Action
Three options, priced: minimum order value for free shipping, exclude the best seller from the welcome code, or accept the loss as paid acquisition — it works out to $1.90 per new customer, which is cheap if they come back. This is a decision, not a fix.

Avoidable spend

$8,790 OBSERVED

Not lost margin — cash going out the door for nothing. Shown as observed, over the period it was actually observed. Not annualised.

−$8,790OBSERVED, 5 MO

One ad set spent $9,200 and brought back $410.

Objective set to link clicks, not purchases, since 3 March · $9,200 − $410 = $8,790 over 5 months · $1,758/mo, still running

ONE SETTING
WORKING PAPER
Category
Avoidable spend. This is not contribution loss — it is cash paid to a platform. Different kind of money, so it is not added to the confirmed-loss column.
Source
Meta Ads Manager (spend, objective, change history) + Shopify (attributed orders). The objective change is timestamped in the account's own edit log.
Period
5 months, 3 March to 31 July. Deliberately not annualised — the set has not run a year, so a yearly figure would be invented. At the observed rate it costs $1,758 a month for as long as it stays on.
Formula
$9,200 spend − $410 attributed sales
= $8,790 over 5 months
÷ 5 = $1,758 / month continuing
Confidence
Spend: high — it is billed. Return: medium — attribution is 7-day-click, and iOS under-reports, so true sales are probably somewhat above $410. That would make the gap smaller, not larger. The number is stated the way that is least flattering to us.
Missing
Whether the set drove any view-through or in-store sales. Nothing in the data can rule that out.
Also found
The destination page was out of stock for 21 days in May. Some of the spend had nowhere to convert.
Action
Switch the objective to purchases or pause it. Do not judge the creative until it has run once against the right objective.

$8,412DISPUTABLE NOW

14 deduction lines totalling $8,412 across 6 POs · carrier scans show full pallet counts on all 6 · every line still inside the 90-day window — the earliest closes in 38 days

READY TO FILE
WORKING PAPER
Category
Recoverable cash, one-time. Not annual leakage — though if the pattern repeats, next year's audit will find it again.
Source
Distributor remittance PDFs (the deduction codes and amounts), matched line-by-line to 3PL bills of lading and carrier delivery scans.

$13,100/YR IF IT CHURNS

Wholesale has been slowing for five months. DTC growth was hiding it.

Largest quiet account: $3,200/mo through last year, reorder cycle stretched 31 → 58 days · $3,200 × 12 × 34% wholesale contribution = $13,056

CALL DRAFTED
WORKING PAPER
Category
Revenue at risk, modelled. Not a loss. It becomes one only if the account actually leaves.
Source
Shopify/invoice history by wholesale account; contribution rate from the wholesale price list against unit COGS.
Formula
$3,200/mo × 12 = $38,400 gross
× 34% wholesale contribution
= $13,056 → $13,100
Assumption
This assumes total churn — the whole account, gone. That is the ceiling, not the expectation. A stretched reorder cycle is a warning, not a departure.
Confidence
Low to medium. The slowdown is fact: repeat share across wholesale fell 61% → 38%, and this account's cycle nearly doubled. The dollar figure attached to it is a model.
Missing
Whether they are buying from someone else, carrying stock, or having their own slow quarter. No data you own can answer that. A phone call can.
Action
Call script drafted — three questions, none of them "is everything okay?"

$1,100–4,400/YR MODELLED

41 subscribers hit pause and never came back. Nobody emailed them.

Paused >90 days · win-backs sent: 0 · avg $17.10/mo × 52% contribution = $8.89/mo each · ceiling 41 × $8.89 × 12 = $4,374

WIN-BACK DRAFTED
WORKING PAPER
Category
Modelled opportunity. The weakest number on this board, and shown as a range for that reason.
Source
Subscription app export (pause dates, plan value) + ESP send history, which shows zero win-back campaigns to this segment.
Formula
ceiling — everyone returns, stays 12 months:
41 × $8.89 × 12 = $4,374

floor — 25% win-back rate:
10 × $8.89 × 12 = $1,067
Why a range
The ceiling assumes every paused subscriber returns and stays a full year. Nobody gets that. A single number here would be marketing, so you get both ends and the assumption behind each.
Margin
Contribution, not revenue: $17.10 plan value × 52% after product, pack and shipping. The gross figure would be $8,413 and would mean less.
Confidence
Low. The 25% floor is a category norm, not your number — you have never run a win-back, so there is no baseline to draw from. The first campaign creates the benchmark.
Action
Win-back sequence drafted. Treat the first send as the measurement, not the payoff.

+17ORDERS / POST

Recipe posts sell more than product shots. You made three last quarter.

Avg orders per post — recipe: 22 · product shot: 5 · difference: +17 · 3 of your 19 posts last quarter were recipes

TEST IT
WORKING PAPER
Category
Unverified opportunity. Counted in no total.
Formula
recipe avg 22 orders − product-shot avg 5
= +17 incremental orders per post

(22 is the average, not the gain —
the gain is the difference.)
Confidence
Low, and the reason matters. Recipe posts may simply have had more reach, better timing, or paid amplification behind them. This board cannot separate format from distribution.
The comeback
If a competitor's analyst wanted to knock this down, they would say: "your recipe posts went out on Sundays with budget behind them; of course they sold more." They would be right that nothing here rules it out.
What would settle it
Four recipe posts and four product shots, matched for day, time and spend. Two months. Then the number means something.
Action
Run the test. Do not rebuild the content calendar on one quarter of unmatched data.
