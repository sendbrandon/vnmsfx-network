An invented company, audited for real. This is what a VNMSFX Audit reads like—the story of a brand that does the hard part beautifully and loses the easy part, and exactly what one week in their records caught.

Uncle Ott’s Horseradish is fictional. Every person, record, date and count below is invented to demonstrate the audit format. A real Audit looks at one agreed area at a time and only at the records agreed with the client.

The story

Uncle Ott’s is two siblings, their grandfather’s recipe, and eight years of grating fresh horseradish that makes grown chefs sit down. It started at a farmers-market card table. It is now 41 grocery doors built store by store, one regional distributor, a webshop shipping 3-packs nationwide, and the Root Club — 214 members who get a quarterly box and whatever the siblings are testing that season.

The product wins awards. The office is a laptop next to the grating room, an inbox with 4,000 unread emails, and a binder labeled DISTRIBUTOR that lives in the delivery van. It is the only office where crying at your email might just be the horseradish.

The recipe has never missed. The follow-through misses plenty.

They brought in an audit because revenue looked fine — and the bank account disagreed.

FINDING 01Wholesale · quiet account

The grocer reordered every three weeks for two years. Then it didn’t.

9 WEEKSof silence from a clockwork account
What the records show

34 consecutive reorders from one specialty grocer, every 19 to 24 days — then nothing for 9 weeks. The account still says active. The last recorded contact is a text message from March, on the older sibling’s phone.

The store changed buyers in the spring; nobody told the new buyer the horseradish wasn’t discontinued. And no chart ever dipped — two new accounts opened the same month, so total wholesale looked fine while the best account went dark.

Why it hurts

Two years of demos and deliveries built that account. It didn’t leave angry — it just faded, while everyone was busy with the doors that still order.

What VNMSFX would build

A system that flags any account past its own ordering rhythm and shows the last order, the history, the owner and a drafted check-in — a weekly list of warm accounts, before they cool all the way.

FINDING 02Ads · direct to consumer

The ads worked. The flavor first-timers buy was gone.

6 DAYSof paid clicks with no flavor a first-timer would pick
What the records show

The ads ran to a page that said in stock — and it was: Extra Hot was in stock. But the Classic, the flavor 9 out of 10 first-time buyers choose, sold out on a Tuesday and stayed out for six days. Clicks continued. First orders stopped. Nothing flagged it, because technically nothing was broken.

Separately: a HOLIDAY code from December is still live in month five, listed on two coupon sites, and stacking with the Root Club discount.

Why it hurts

A cold customer does not start their horseradish journey at Extra Hot. The page looked fine, the dashboard blamed the creative, and the ad budget kept buying visits with nothing a first-timer would pick.

What VNMSFX would build

A system that checks active campaigns against the flavor and size new customers actually buy — its availability, the variant mix on the page, offer dates and code rules — not just whether the page loads. It flags the mismatch while the ads are still spending.

FINDING 03Social · what to post

The posts that sell jars are the posts they stopped making.

3 VS 40recipe reels posted last quarter, versus label glamour shots
What the records show

Three recipe reels posted last quarter — grandma’s remoulade, a bloody-mary build, a sinus-clearing first-taste reaction. Each one lines up with a bump in site visits and first orders. The forty label glamour shots don’t.

Nobody tracks any of it, so the content calendar keeps favoring what looks good in the grid.

Why it hurts

The content that sells is being rationed. The content that doesn’t is being produced on schedule. The effort lands where the likes are — not where the orders are.

What VNMSFX would build

A system that classifies every post by format, lines each format up against an agreed traffic-and-order window, separates real attribution from coincidence, and tells the calendar which formats deserve another test.

FINDING 04Wholesale · deductions

The distributor deducted for short cases. The proof was in three places.

9 DAYSuntil the first dispute deadline closes
What the records show

Across four purchase orders this quarter, the distributor deducted for 17 cases marked short. The purchase orders, the 3PL pick records and the signed delivery receipts — which show full counts on three of the four shipments — live in email, a portal and the binder in the van.

The dispute window is 90 days. The earliest deadline closes in 9 days. Nobody has matched the paperwork, because the person who could is running the grater.

Why it hurts

No single deduction was big enough for anyone to stop and look — that is exactly why they add up. The evidence to push back exists; it just rides around in the van until the deadline makes it worthless.

What VNMSFX would build

A system that reads each deduction, checks it against the dispute deadline, gathers the matching purchase order, pick record and delivery receipt, and prepares one evidence package for the siblings to review.

FINDING 05The Root Club · win-backs

Every lapsed member gets the same 20% off — whatever the reason they left.

20%the one offer sent to every canceled or paused member
What the records show

The Root Club win-back is one email: 20% off, sent to everyone. But the records show who left for which reason — nine members lapsed because a reissued card failed, several paused over shipping cost, and some simply chose to leave. The offer ignores all of it.

Why it hurts

The failed-card members would come back for a simple update-your-card note. The shipping-sensitive ones would come back for a shipping discount. They all get 20% off instead — the deepest discount, spent on the people who needed it least.

What VNMSFX would build

A system that separates lapsed members by why they left and matches the approved offer to the reason — card note, shipping discount, smaller coupon, the full 20% only where it earns its keep — then compares each approach against actual reactivation.
