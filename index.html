01The problem

Nothing looks broken. That's the problem.

Operating problems that cost you time or money rarely show up in one place. One piece may be in your order history, another in your inbox, warehouse records or store reports. By the time someone connects the pieces, those problems may already be expensive or urgent.

An established retailer goes quiet.

They normally order every 30 to 45 days. Now it has been 80. The last order is in one tool and the person responsible for the account is in another, so weeks pass without a follow-up.

The ad platform says a campaign worked. Your store says otherwise.

Both reports look believable. Without comparing the same date range and what each report counts as a sale, you may keep funding the wrong campaign—or stop one that worked.

A distributor pays less and says cases were missing.

The claim is in a PDF, the purchase order is in email and the warehouse record is in another portal. If nobody connects them before the deadline, you may lose the chance to challenge it.

The retailer ordered. The warehouse never got the instruction.

Sales thinks the order is moving, but the warehouse has no instruction. The ship date passes quietly, putting a sale you already won at risk.

03The method in practice

NYC’s #1-rated vegan chocolate company ran its biggest giveaway yet—with one system keeping the work visible.

Doshi, PATH Water, Blackbird Foods and 15 other vendors were part of Sweet Vegan’s annual Harvest Basket. The team had to track who donated what, when shipments would arrive, every shoot detail and every follow-up before one launch date.

The work lived across inboxes and memory. One unanswered email could mean missing product, a late shoot or a vendor left waiting until the launch was already under pressure.

I built one system that showed what was confirmed, what had arrived, what was still missing and who needed to act next. The team could ask where the campaign stood and see the full picture immediately.

With the system in place, Sweet Vegan completed its biggest Harvest Basket yet. The campaign got bigger without becoming harder to control.

This was an earlier vendor follow-up system—not a Leak Audit or a recovered-money case study. It shows the same VNMSFX method: make unfinished work visible while there is still time to act.
