01Direct-to-consumer brands spending on paid media

AAds pointing at what you can’t ship

What happens before the workflow

The ad keeps spending — but the product is sold out, the page is broken, or the promotion in the ad has expired.

How the brand loses money

You pay for clicks that cannot complete the purchase, and frustrate customers who were ready to buy.

How the workflow fixes it

It checks active campaigns against product pages, inventory and offer dates, and flags mismatches so your team can pause, redirect or correct.

BDiscounts that stack past the margin

What happens before the workflow

An influencer code was supposed to end after a weekend. Six months later it is still active and on coupon sites — or customers stack discounts that were never meant to combine.

How the brand loses money

The business gives away more discount than management approved.

How the workflow fixes it

It compares code usage with approved dates and rules, shows the affected orders and discount amounts, and routes the code to its owner for review.

02Wholesale-heavy brands

ADistributor deductions and shortages

What happens before the workflow

The distributor pays less, claiming 100 cases were missing. The deduction is in a PDF, the purchase order in email, the warehouse record in the 3PL portal — and nobody matches them before the dispute deadline expires.

How the brand loses money

The distributor simply pays less. The records may support a dispute, but the chance is lost because no one assembled the evidence in time.

How the workflow fixes it

It reads each deduction, checks the dispute window, gathers the purchase order, warehouse record and delivery proof, and prepares the evidence package. Your team decides whether to dispute.

BOrders that stall before they ship

What happens before the workflow

A retailer sends a purchase order. Sales believes it is accepted — but inventory is short, the warehouse never got the instruction, the ship date passes, and nobody tells the retailer.

How the brand loses money

The retailer may cancel, cut future orders, or issue a shortage deduction. The order was already won; the internal handoff failed.

How the workflow fixes it

It compares accepted orders, inventory, warehouse allocation, ship dates and delivered quantities — and flags what is late, short or missing so a person can act before the retailer does.

03Subscription brands

AFailed payments that never recover

What happens before the workflow

A subscriber’s card expires. The retry is weak, the email never sends, nobody sees high-value failures — and the subscription eventually cancels.

How the brand loses money

The customer did not choose to leave. The transaction failed, and the recovery process never completed.

How the workflow fixes it

It shows the failed charge, subscriber history, retry status and the next approved recovery step. Your team handles the customer.

BSubscription says active, shipping says no

What happens before the workflow

The app says active, but no charge occurred. The payment succeeded, but no order was created. The customer canceled, but marketing still emails them.

How the brand loses money

You can lose revenue, ship unpaid product, or fail to ship paid product — failures between systems that no single dashboard shows.

How the workflow fixes it

It checks the chain — payment → order → fulfillment — and when a step is missing, flags it with the records and the person who owns it.

04Sales and accounts (any channel)

AAccounts that go quiet

What happens before the workflow

A retailer used to order every 30 to 45 days. It has now been 80 — but nobody owns a reorder report, and the CRM does not know the last actual order date.

How the brand loses money

The retailer may have forgotten, changed buyers, or started buying from a competitor. You already paid the cost of winning the account, then let the relationship quietly die.

How the workflow fixes it

It shows the account, last order, normal reorder pattern, owner and a suggested follow-up — a weekly list of warm accounts that deserve attention.

BFollow-ups that never went out

What happens before the workflow

A buyer asks for samples at a trade show. A quote goes out with no reminder. A broker introduction sits in an inbox and never enters a shared pipeline.

How the brand loses money

You spend on trade shows, samples, brokers and travel — and qualified opportunities disappear because nobody owns the next action.

How the workflow fixes it

One review list answering one question: which buyer inquiries, samples, quotes or introductions have no owner or an overdue next step?

These are not dashboards. They catch what needs attention.

“This account, order, payment, campaign, deduction, subscriber or buyer conversation needs attention — and here is the evidence explaining why.”

Your team still decides what to do. The system’s job is to make sure the important item does not stay hidden until the money, the customer, the dispute window or the opportunity is gone.
